# 2024 verification record

The catalog contains 17 individual problems from Kathmandu, Nepal, comprising
12 theory questions, three sky-chart tasks, and two telescope tasks. All 17
entries have matching official solution material in the supplied combined
answer-and-solution PDF.

## Sources and version choice

The checks use physical PDF page numbers, starting at 1.

- Theory questions and the selected observation questions come from
  Theory/Junior_IOAA_2024_question_papers.pdf, a 26-page combined file.
- Official solutions, observation answer sheets, and marked charts come from
  Theory/Junior_IOAA_2024_answer_sheets_and_solutions.pdf, a 29-page file.
- Observation (Sky Chart)/IOAAJr_2024_Q13-15.pdf is a separate three-page
  observation version. Its OC1 is worth 43.5 points and its OC2 has nine
  pictures worth 13.5 points. With OC3, the round totals 75 points.
- The selected combined version gives OC1 45 points, OC2 six pictures worth
  9 points, and OC3 18 points, totalling 72 points. It matches the supplied
  answer sheets and key. Both catalog notes and source comments identify the
  version difference.
- Observation (Telescope)/IOAAJr_2024_Q16-17.pdf was compared with the matching
  telescope material in the combined file. It gives the same 32-point Moon
  task and 16-point planet task.

The separate group competition in the combined file is outside this
individual-problem collection.
All four supplied 2024 PDF files were independently checked against the Git
blob checksums in the pinned source manifest.

The relevant supplied constants from question pp. 2–4 are included with the
affected entries. Their editable text was checked against the original
constant rows. The source crops for T3 and T5 include the Solar System table
column header before the Earth row so the numerical columns remain labelled.

The following source issues are retained and identified by visible catalog
notes and source comments.

- T1's printed extinction magnitude equation has a sign inconsistent with its
  subsequent iteration formula.
- T2's final speed ratio is the reciprocal of the perihelion-to-aphelion ratio
  implied by the question order.
- T3 multiplies by cos(latitude) in its longitude calculation and gives a
  Ranchi watch time inconsistent with the stated time-zone offsets.
- T5 says prime meridian in the question while its solution assumes the
  satellite is on Kathmandu's local meridian.
- T8's rise-time calculation assumes local-noon culmination without using the
  supplied equinox epoch, date, or right ascension.
- T9 omits a mass factor when subtracting a specific internal potential in its
  escape-energy equation.
- T12 says that 50 K dust re-emits UV light while its official peak wavelength
  lies in the infrared.

## Observation checks

OC1 uses question p. 11 and answer-sheet pp. 14–15. Its solution is on pp. 17–21.
The seven subparts, requested object counts, point values, map markings,
four-star order, and Aldebaran coordinate tolerance table were compared.
The question gives part 7 three points, but the key calls 2.5 points full
credit. The book records this discrepancy.

OC2 uses question p. 12, answer-sheet p. 16, and solution p. 22. The six-picture
version and all category entries match the key. Picture 5 is the asteroid,
picture 2 the dwarf planet, and pictures 1, 3, 4, and 6 the satellites, with
1.5 points per classification.

OC3 uses question p. 13, answer-sheet p. 16, and solution pp. 22–23. All six
object images, Messier numbers, constellations, the M42/M43 alternative, and
the 1.5-point allocations were checked.

OT1 uses question pp. 15–16, answer-sheet p. 24, and solution pp. 26–27. The
pointing limit, two viewing intervals, lunar chart, drawing/cardinal-point
marks, crater/mare allocations, and age tolerances were compared. The
official 8.3-day age, Southern hemisphere, and Taurus answers are retained.
The live telescope view is represented by its surviving official solution
image.

OT2 uses question p. 15, answer-sheet p. 25, and solution pp. 27–28. The
three-day animation instruction, allowed planet choices, consistent moon
labels, point split, and descending size order Ganymede, Callisto, Io, Europa
match. The original animation is absent from the supplied files, and the
statement says that the initial and final views survive in the key.

## Figures and layout review

All 15 figure recipes were checked against PDF page bounds and rendered in a
contact sheet. This covers the Cepheid plot, both binary figures, the blank sky
chart and four marked overlays, six Solar System pictures, deep-sky images,
lunar chart/views, and both Jupiter views. Labels and chart borders are intact.

The final integrated LaTeX edition has 117 pages and the crop edition has
170 pages. Representative 2024 theory entries, observation charts, long notes,
telescope images, chapter headings, contents, and year-index pages were
rendered and inspected after the final rebuild. The samples show intact
metadata, formulas, figure labels, note text, and table borders, with no
clipping or overlapping heading rules. The source-crop Earth data now retain
their column header. All 17 LaTeX problem/solution pairs were independently
checked to link to their matching named destinations.

This review tightened the first Jupiter-view figure crop from y = 412 to
y = 415 PDF points to exclude the clipped bottom of the preceding caption.
It also restored T10's first velocity-ratio sentence to the exact printed
relation v1/v2 = m2/m1, replacing an equivalent reciprocal formulation.
