Radu is observing the night sky from Aveiro ($\varphi=40.67^\circ$ N, $L=8.65^\circ$ W) in the year 2024. During his observations, he sees Altair ($\alpha_{\mathrm{Altair}}=19^{\mathrm h}51^{\mathrm m}$, $\delta_{\mathrm{Altair}}=8^\circ55'50''$) rising at local solar time $T_{\mathrm{Local},1}=22{:}39{:}43$. Later, at local solar time $T_{\mathrm{Local},2}=02{:}42{:}58$, he observes that Altair and Arcturus ($\delta_{\mathrm{Arcturus}}=19^\circ33'6''$) are at the same altitude. The observations are made from sea level. Ignore atmospheric effects.
\begin{description}
\item[(a)] Determine the sidereal time at the moment Altair rises.
\item[(b)] Determine the current date (at the moment Altair rises). Ignore the equation of time, and consider that the right ascension of the Sun is increasing uniformly through the year, at a rate of $3^{\mathrm m}56^{\mathrm s}$ per day. The spring equinox occurs on March 21st.
\item[(c)] Determine the right ascension of Arcturus. Take into account that Arcturus is in the Western hemisphere of the sky at $T_{\mathrm{Local},2}$.
\end{description}
In your solution, use $a$ for altitude, $H$ for hour angle, $\delta$ for declination, and $\alpha$ for right ascension.
