In a classical approximation to get a feel for expanding universe, one may assume that the universe is uniformly filled cloud with small particles, and that the critical density $\rho_c$ is the density of the cloud at which the expansion of the universe (the Hubble--Lema\^itre law) stops at infinite time.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Derive a classical expression for $\rho_c$ of the universe.
\item Find the value of critical density in SI units.
\item If the universe was filled with tennis balls of mass $0.145\,\mathrm{kg}$ each distributed uniformly (no other mass) and the mass density of this universe equal to the critical density, then how many tennis balls would be included in a sphere of size of the Sun?
\end{enumerate}
\textit{Supplied examination data.} $H_0=70\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$, $G=6.674\times10^{-11}\,\mathrm{N\,m^2\,kg^{-2}}$, $1\,\mathrm{pc}=3.086\times10^{16}\,\mathrm{m}$, and $R_\odot=695500\,\mathrm{km}$.
