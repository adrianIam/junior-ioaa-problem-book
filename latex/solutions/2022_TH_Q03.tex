Let $v_B$ be the speed of body B at launch, relative to the Sun, such that
the body reaches the limit of the Sun's gravitational attraction at rest.
\ioaafig{2022_TH_Q03-s1.pdf}
Conservation of mechanical energy gives
\[
\frac{mv_B^2}{2}-K\frac{mM_E}{R_E}-K\frac{mM_S}{R_{ES}}=0.
\]
Here $K$ denotes the gravitational constant. With the supplied assumption
$M_E/R_E\ll M_S/R_{ES}$,
\[
\frac{mv_B^2}{2}-K\frac{mM_S}{R_{ES}}=0,\qquad
v_B=\sqrt{\frac{2KM_S}{R_{ES}}}=\sqrt2\,V_0.
\]
The Earth's circular orbital speed is
\[
V_0=\frac{2\pi R_{ES}}{T_{ES}}\approx30\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}},\qquad
v_B\approx42.42\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}.
\]
The trajectory relative to the Sun is parabolic, with the Sun at its focus.
The answer is (d).
