\begin{enumerate}
\item We know
\[\sin a=\sin\phi\sin\delta+\cos\phi\cos\delta\cos H.\]
\hfill(3 points)
At rise, $a=0$, giving $\cos H=-\tan\phi\tan\delta$. \hfill(1 point)
Solve for $H$ to get 5 hours 49 minutes 59 seconds. \hfill(3 points)
Then $t_{\mathrm{rise}}=12\,\mathrm{pm}-H=6{:}10\,\mathrm{am}$. \hfill(2 points)
% sic: The official key assumes local-noon culmination and does not use the supplied equinox epoch, date or right ascension. Its stated rise time is retained.
\item Since the stars are very close, use planar geometry,
\[d=\sqrt{(\Delta\alpha)^2+(\Delta\delta)^2}.\]
\hfill(1 point)
Substitute $\Delta\alpha=0.08376\,\mathrm{rad}$ \hfill(2 points)
and $\Delta\delta=0.01927\,\mathrm{rad}$. \hfill(2 points)
This gives $d=0.08595\,\mathrm{rad}$, or $4.92^\circ$. \hfill(1 point)
Answers rounding to $4.9^\circ$ are acceptable. Answers in degrees or subunits thereof for any part are acceptable. Correct solutions with spherical trigonometry also receive full points.
\end{enumerate}
